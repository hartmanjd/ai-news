01 Hacker News

All AI-generated code should be Open Source

The author argues that all code generated by AI should be released as Open Source. They say AI models were trained on Open Source code and on Stack Overflow content under Creative Commons licenses that require attribution, so turning that knowledge into closed proprietary code amounts to license laundering. They also argue that purely AI-generated code is not written by a human and therefore should not be copyrightable, citing the Monkey Selfie case and the U.S. Copyright Office. Because copyright rules differ by country, such as the UK granting limited protection to computer-generated works, the author says developers and companies should release AI-generated code under Open Source licenses or dedicate it to the public domain with CC0. The author adds that code produced with little human effort beyond writing prompts should definitely be Open Source.

02 Hacker News

Understanding Positional Embeddings in Modern LLMs

This article explains how large language models encode and process information about token position, covering classic methods and newer ones like RoPE (rotary position embeddings). It walks through the mechanisms with visual examples, some math, and a small PyTorch implementation, and also looks at how modern architectures adapt or omit explicit positional encodings. The author wrote it as a standalone foundation for an upcoming article on LLM architecture trends, and to answer long-standing reader requests for a RoPE tutorial. The article notes that GPT-2 adds a learned position vector to each token embedding before the first transformer block, while newer models like MiniMax-M3 add positional information inside attention using RoPE. It also explains why explicit position matters: without it, swapping words like "red" and "gray" would produce the same embeddings and attention scores, though causal masks provide some implicit order information. The piece is technical and detailed, and the author says readers already familiar with position embeddings can skip to Section 6 on positional choices in modern architectures.

03 TechCrunch

Where was Nvidia’s security team?

Nvidia’s longtime chief security officer, David Reber, stepped down in August, citing health reasons, and was replaced by Saša Zdjelar. Two and a half months later, CEO Jensen Huang left the company amid questions about extramarital relationships, including one with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore data center company that U.S. investigators suspect of funneling Nvidia chips to China. Nvidia’s top lawyer had been engaged with the government’s Megaspeed inquiry more than a year earlier, but it is unclear whether the security team knew about the investigation or Huang’s relationships. Security specialists say affairs can give foreign intelligence services leverage over executives, and that security teams are often underfunded and narrowly focused on facilities and cyberattacks rather than insider threats. The article raises the question of what Nvidia’s security team knew and whether it could have acted, which may become clearer as the board’s independent investigation and federal inquiries proceed.

04 TechCrunch

Elon Musk, other tech execs sign petition calling on Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang

A petition asking Nvidia to reinstate its recently ousted CEO Jensen Huang has gathered more than 115,000 signatures, including from Elon Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan. The petition says signers include 5,965 current Nvidia employees, 1,108 former employees, and 63,063 shareholders, and it argues Huang was removed without meaningful due process and should return while an independent investigation takes place. Huang resigned on Thursday, four days after admitting to an affair with a chip executive suspected by U.S. intelligence of being a Chinese spy. He has agreed to testify before Congress, and reports also suggest he had multiple extramarital affairs. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while it searches for a permanent replacement. The company's stock fell sharply during the week, and Huang's departure is expected to leave a big gap in AI industry funding.

05 The Verge

Congress wants to tighten export rules on AI chips after Nvidia spy scandal

Congress is moving to tighten export rules on AI chips after a scandal involving former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and a suspected Chinese spy. Both Democrats and Republicans support measures to restrict chip exports, and lawmakers have demanded that Huang and other Nvidia leaders testify before Congress. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Huang and the Trump administration need to answer to Congress, and Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott sent letters to Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick calling for public testimony. Bills like the Remote Access Security Act, introduced in the House in April 2025 but stalled in the Senate, are getting renewed interest. House Speaker Mike Johnson said there is "growing consensus in Congress on limiting chip exports to China" and that he personally supports restricting China's access to powerful AI chips from companies like Nvidia.

An overview of Jensen Huang’s messy days

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia on November 12th after a scandal that began when The New York Times reported he had a close relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed who US officials suspected was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on him. Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on November 8th, and Nvidia's board launched an investigation; days later, the Times reported a second affair with Kimberly Powell, an Nvidia vice president and one of his direct reports. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO, and Harvey Jones became the company's first board chair. Nvidia's stock fell from an all-time high of $239.24 on October 6th to $162.85 on November 13th. The company is central to the US AI industry and its competition with China, having invested $99 billion in AI labs and startups and announced plans to mobilize over $500 billion for AI data centers and other infrastructure.
