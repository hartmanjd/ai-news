02 Hacker News

Chinese frontier AI models carry the fingerprint of export-controlled GPUs

A forensic analysis of publicly released model weights found that six models from four Chinese AI labs—DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group—contain numerical patterns matching NVIDIA’s Blackwell GPUs, which U.S. export rules restrict from being sold to China. The models, released between April and November 2026, include DeepSeek-V4-Flash and V4-Pro, Kimi K3 and K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3 and Ling-3.1-flash; earlier models from the same labs matched the older Hopper generation. The researchers recovered traces of each model’s initial values and matched them to specific Blackwell configurations, confirming the match on physical chips for two models, and ruled out AMD hardware as an alternative for one of them. The findings offer technical evidence that these labs had access to advanced chips, though they do not show where the chips were located, how access was obtained, or whether export controls were broken. The authors estimate the largest training runs needed thousands of Blackwell GPUs over two to three months, costing hundreds of millions of dollars, and note that other Chinese labs such as Alibaba, Zhipu and Tencent still read as Hopper-trained.

Edge0-145B: Frontier AI Capabilities on Your Own Computer

Edge0 has open-sourced two on-device AI models, Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B, along with its inference framework and deployment tools. 04 Hacker News

AI unlocks Gödel's notebooks, said to contain hundreds of proved theorems

A project at Helsinki University led by logician Jan von Plato has used the AI model Claude to read Kurt Gödel's 1940s workbooks, 1,009 pages written in an obsolete German shorthand that humans had managed to decipher at only about a page a week. Within days, Claude produced a 650-page digest showing that Gödel had proved hundreds of theorems no one knew about, including 663 theorems that were then checked with the proof assistant Coq. About 400 were verified as stated, about 200 needed repair, and about 50 were false. Claude also reconstructed a near-complete proof of the independence of the axiom of choice, which Gödel had claimed in 1966 that he already had but could not reproduce. Von Plato says the workbooks contain dozens of results anticipated decades early and 14 logic themes never pursued, suggesting Gödel discovered significantly more than anyone had thought.

A surge in AI-generated apps has overwhelmed Apple's App Store review process, causing delays and rejections for music app developers. 10 Ars Technica

OpenAI board member worries an AI "takeover" of society could be months away

Paul Cristiano, a board member of the OpenAI Foundation and head of the Alignment Research Center, gave a lecture in Toronto warning about the risk of an "AI takeover." He defined this as a situation where goal-seeking AI systems control what happens on Earth, and humans trying to stop them would be as ineffective as trying to stop the US military. He said such a scenario could lead to mass death and possibly make the environment uninhabitable for humans. Cristiano admitted there is no quantitative model to put a number on this risk, but said that on the fastest development trajectory, humanity faces "something like a coin flip" of an AI takeover, with a meaningful risk in the next six to 12 months. He pointed to this summer's OpenAI/Hugging Face hacking incident as an example of what might be seen shortly before something really bad happens.

11 Ars Technica

FAA expands AI advice to manage air traffic congestion across entire US airspace

The Federal Aviation Administration has expanded its AI tool called SMART to help manage air traffic across all 22 air traffic control centers in the US, after an initial rollout in the Washington, DC area on September 21. The FAA says the $875 million system helped reroute 180 flights there, saving an average of 10 minutes and nearly 80 gallons of fuel per flight, and it hopes to repeat those results nationwide. SMART is a cloud-based system that combines weather, flight path, traffic flow and staffing data to predict traffic patterns and recommend schedules, routes and arrival orders. Airline executives support the expansion, but the union representing air traffic controllers says it was not involved in designing or testing the system. A team of FAA specialists in DC must validate SMART's recommendations before they reach controllers.
