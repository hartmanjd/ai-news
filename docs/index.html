01 Hacker News

Backdoor in Claude Code for VS Code extension on the Cursor Marketplace

Phorion found a backdoor in version 2.1.313 of the official Claude Code for VS Code extension on the Cursor Marketplace. The compromised extension adds an attacker-controlled SSH key to the user's authorized_keys file and opens a reverse SSH tunnel to an attacker server, running as soon as the extension loads without any user action. Cursor has removed the release from its marketplace and pulled the NPM and VS Code versions from their registries. On macOS, a bug in the malware's setup logic means it often fails to turn on Remote Login, which can block inbound access unless SSH was already enabled. Organisations should check for use of version 2.1.313 and start incident response if it is found.

02 Hacker News

We ran AI agent swarms in Minecraft and Mindustry

Researchers teamed up with Olam Labs to run experiments with swarms of AI agents in the games Minecraft and Mindustry, to study how large groups of agents coordinate. In one Minecraft test, 50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents spent three hours trying to "build a thriving civilization," and they formed a hierarchy, wrote rules, and sent about 5,900 messages. After 22 of their 34 deaths happened on the first night, the agents became much more cautious and focused on food and survival instead of building. They set up a rationing system and a fishing operation with assigned roles like fishermen, cooks, and couriers. In a separate series of nine Bed Wars matches, teams of 10 Claude Opus 5.5 agents played against 10 GPT-6.1 Sol agents, and the GPT team won 6–3. The researchers say studying these swarms matters because multi-agent systems can produce both powerful and dangerous emergent behavior, and they open-sourced the Minecraft tool they built for the tests.

03 Hacker News

AI isn't replacing SREs, it's deskilling them

A newsletter from SigNoz argues that AI is not replacing site reliability engineers (SREs) but is quietly deskilling them. It draws on a 1983 paper by psychologist Lisanne Bainbridge, "Ironies of Automation," which found that the more a process is automated, the less practice humans get at the rare failures that matter most. The article says AI already handles alert grouping, runbook execution, anomaly detection, and basic root-cause suggestions, with more autonomy coming. It cites studies in medicine, where endoscopists using AI saw unassisted polyp detection drop from 28% to 22%, and in aviation, where the FAA now requires manual flying time. The concern is that SREs who only handle novel incidents lose the hands-on practice that builds intuition, and junior engineers may never develop it at all.

06 TechCrunch

Google’s first data center satellite is online and writing poetry

Google's first data center satellite, part of its Suncatcher project, is now online and communicating with the ground after launching on a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket. The satellite carries four Google Tensor Processing Units and generates up to 1 kW of electricity, but it can only run one TPU at a time for about 20 minutes due to power demands. After about three weeks of testing, engineers sent the first prompt to Google DeepMind's Gemma 4 model onboard, asking it to write a haiku about space, and also had it report on orbital conditions. A Google spokesperson said the satellite is operating nominally, and the team will now study how the chips handle thermal stress and space radiation, which can cause "bit flips" that corrupt data. If the TPUs perform well, Google plans to launch two more satellites with the chips in 2027 to demonstrate inter-satellite optical links.

09 Ars Technica

Fujifilm to boost tape production 5x as AI fuels archiving demand

Fujifilm announced it will invest about 10 billion yen (roughly $63 million) to increase its magnetic tape production capacity fivefold compared with fiscal year 2025 levels. The company says the spread of generative AI is causing "exponential growth" in data needs worldwide, which is driving demand for tape as an archiving medium. The investment will expand its Kanagawa Factory's Odawara site from fiscal years 2026 through 2028, mainly to produce its latest-generation LTO Ultrium10 tape. Fujifilm says major cloud service and data center operators are using more tape because it stores large amounts of data at relatively low cost. The company also notes that tape cartridges can be disconnected and stored separately, which helps protect data from threats such as ransomware.

10 Ars Technica

Thousands of “ghost students” use AI to steal millions in student loans, DOJ says

The US Department of Justice has charged five people in its first major federal crackdown on "ghost students," fraudsters who steal Americans' identities to pose as fake college students and take student loans. Officials said the scheme relied partly on AI, which fraudsters used to fill out applications, complete coursework, and make it look like fake students were attending online classes. The DOJ seized more than $750,000 in cryptocurrency from a network linked to Kenyan nationals, and officials said they identified over 830,000 potentially fraudulent federal student aid accounts, with losses possibly near $130 million. The scheme targeted people who never attended college, leaving victims unaware until the government came to collect on the loans years later. The case matters because it shows how AI is being used to scale identity fraud against taxpayer-funded programs, while new automated identity checks meant to stop the fraud may also delay or block real low-income students from getting aid.
