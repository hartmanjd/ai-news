01 Hacker News

Goodbye Claude, hello Pi

A longtime Claude Code user has decided to stop using it as their main coding tool and switch to Pi, an open-source alternative. The author cites many small frustrations, including having to copy or symlink between CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md files, a bug where only one session can connect to the IDE at a time, and problems with the official VSCode extension. They also criticize Anthropic for shipping features quickly without attention to quality or stability, noting 244 releases this year, over three-quarters of which changed the system prompt or tool descriptions. The author says Claude Code lacks a strong product vision and that Pi offers more stability, a minimalist approach, and customizability. The article notes that Anthropic announced AGENTS.md support just a day after the author decided to leave, which they say was too late.

02 Hacker News

Llama-modchip: 284B DeepSeek on 64GB RAM, no GPU

A project called llama-modchip has released software that runs large AI models on ordinary CPUs without graphics cards. It says a 284-billion-parameter version of DeepSeek-V4-Flash, compressed to 56.56 GiB, fits in 64 GB of RAM and runs at roughly 9 to 15 tokens per second, while a 30-billion-parameter Nemotron model reaches up to about 111 tokens per second on a high-end Threadripper workstation. The tool uses custom compression and caching techniques and includes a command-line chat interface, a web UI, and a way to run Claude Code locally. The models use a custom data format, so they will not open in standard llama.cpp, Ollama, or LM Studio; users must run the project's own patched binary. It matters because it suggests frontier-scale models could run on hardware many developers already own, without GPUs or cloud APIs, though the speeds are slower than typical GPU setups.

OpenAI raises $11B at a $1.25T valuation

OpenAI received about $11 billion in new funding by Friday, November 6, 2026, as the first part of a round priced at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, CFO Sarah Friar confirmed. 04 Hacker News

Why I will not be using LLMs in my coding

Kristian Polso, a programmer with about 10 years of experience building production machine learning systems, wrote a blog post explaining why he will not use LLMs or AI agents in his professional coding work. He lists several reasons: studies and his own experience suggest LLM use can cause cognitive "brainfog," the generated code often looks impressive but contains poor or overly complex solutions, and code written by agents becomes hard for humans to maintain. He also cites environmental concerns about AI datacenters, discomfort with US-based AI companies and GDPR and CLOUD Act data risks, and his personal enjoyment of writing code. He acknowledges some positive uses, such as getting unstuck on a problem, but says the help is not worth the drawbacks. The post matters because it adds a detailed, experience-based voice to the debate over whether AI coding tools should be a default part of professional software work.

05 Hacker News

Goldman Sachs Is Pulling Claude Code from Its Bankers and Traders

Goldman Sachs has restricted Anthropic's Claude Code and Microsoft's GitHub to its roughly 12,000 engineers, cutting off most traders and bankers who had used the tools to track client flows, model market positions, and build written and graphic materials. The October guidance followed a review of productivity and risk concerns, and Bloomberg reported that some traders were frustrated by the change. The bank had already barred its Hong Kong bankers from Claude in April over contract and location terms. The restriction applies to third-party agents that can write and run code on their own, while Goldman's in-house AI assistant remains available across the firm. The move matters because unreviewed agent-written code on a trading desk can bypass the review, testing, and information-barrier controls that banks rely on. Goldman is also a lead underwriter on Anthropic's IPO, expected on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT before the end of November.

06 TechCrunch

After the midterms, things are looking rocky for Elon Musk and data centers

Democrats won this week's midterm elections, and the results could affect the tech industry. Voters in many places rejected or restricted data centers, and some winning politicians, like Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, campaigned on limiting them. On a TechCrunch podcast, reporters said Elon Musk donated about half a billion dollars to Republicans but got little in return, and his companies are based in a state that has turned strongly Democratic. They also said data centers have become a grassroots issue because companies often kept plans secret and required officials to sign NDAs. Megaspeed exec was recruited to spy on Nvidia CEO, according to US intelligence memo

A U.S. intelligence memo circulated earlier this year claimed that tech executive Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, according to media reports first led by The New York Times. The Financial Times later reported that the memo also claimed Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, who works at a Singapore-based company called Megaspeed. An Nvidia spokesperson told the NYT that Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were "not unusual" and that the company does not share classified information with customers. A separate NYT report looked at New Matrix, a Chinese company authorized by the U.S. Commerce Department to buy advanced Nvidia chips, but reporters found only "a hollow gray shell" at its listed office address, raising concerns it could be a front for a blacklisted private equity firm. The story matters because it raises questions about Chinese intelligence efforts aimed at Nvidia, one of the world's most important chip companies, and about how advanced U.S. chips reach buyers overseas.

08 The Verge

Elvis and Eminem rights holders are suing Suno

Rights holders for Elvis Presley's music and the publisher of some of Eminem's biggest records are suing the AI music company Suno, claiming it illegally generates music and lyrics that mimic those artists. The Presley lawsuit argues Suno violated Tennessee's ELVIS Act, which makes it illegal for an AI company to clone a singer's voice. The suits are similar to one filed by Jason Isbell and others in September, which claimed Suno made imitations of the plaintiffs without directly claiming copyright infringement. A Suno spokesperson said the company exists to help people create original music, prohibits unauthorized use of artists' work, and blocks prompts for specific artists' names and songs. The new cases add to existing lawsuits against Suno from Sony, UMG, Isbell, Reservoir Media and others.
