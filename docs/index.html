01 Hacker News

Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen

Astra, a frontier language model, can read and write neural network weights directly without using gradient descent, a method the researchers call Weight-Space Programming. Given only raw weights, Astra identified which game four PPO-trained networks were built to play and what shapes three diffusion models generate. It also named the training language of three small language models from their middle layers alone and correctly found their copying heads. Most notably, Astra fixed Qwen3.5-0.8B's tendency to get stuck in reasoning loops by editing just 0.016% of its weights, raising its MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% and tripling IFEval without any gradient-based training. The work suggests LLMs could soon optimize neural network weights by reasoning about them, rather than relying only on symbolic optimizers like Adam.

02 Hacker News

Anthropic's internal AI use, at retail prices, now exceeds its employee pay

Anthropic revealed in its IPO filing that the value of Claude AI used internally, calculated at the prices it charges customers, exceeded its monthly employee compensation costs in April, excluding stock awards. By September, internal AI usage had grown more than 250-fold since January. The company isn't actually spending more cash on AI than on salaries; it is valuing its internal AI consumption at retail prices. More than 90% of the code added to its software in September was written by Claude, and revenue has grown much faster than headcount. The figures suggest AI is challenging the long-held assumption that talented humans are the most valuable resource in Silicon Valley.

Anthropic reported updated financials in an amended S-1 filing with the SEC on Monday as it moves toward a possible IPO. The update gives investors a fuller picture of Anthropic's business as it prepares for its IPO roadshow amid growing market worries about AI spending.

04 Hacker News

China's First Frontier AI Export Controls: Mofcom Announcement No. 48

On October 16, 2026, China's Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology issued Announcement No. 48, which proposes adding 40 technologies to the country's export control catalogue, including 23 in artificial intelligence. The draft would restrict frontier AI model weights, agent architectures, and AI-enabled cyber tools above set technical thresholds, such as models trained with at least 10^26 FLOPs or 2 trillion parameters, while banning some items outright. The catalogue additions are open for comment until November 15, 2026, but a separate pre-export registration requirement is already in effect, covering transfers of ownership, overseas licensing, and foreign control changes. China's commerce ministry said the revision is routine and does not target any specific country or entity. The move matters because it is China's first export control aimed at frontier AI, and it means AI supply chains now face formal outbound controls from both China and the United States.

06 TechCrunch

Anthropic warns that it’s very close to RSI capability, aka self-building AI

Anthropic said in a revised SEC filing on Monday that it may soon meet its own internal definition of recursive self-improvement, or RSI, the point where AI becomes good enough at improving itself to sharply speed up AI progress. The company said it has already used AI to develop new models whose capability jumps it did not recognize at the time, and that future models, not current ones, suggest it may imminently cross that threshold. Anthropic stressed that no current model has crossed it yet, and said it plans to keep humans in control of frontier model development. It also warned that using AI to build more capable AI could pose a serious safety risk if it accelerates progress too fast. The disclosure matters because RSI is seen by some as a path to runaway AI beyond human control, and by others as a source of huge productivity gains.

08 The Verge

Anthropic clamps down on Claude’s AI video game clones

Anthropic has updated the system prompt for Claude Code to stop the tool from recreating existing video games, according to Tom's Hardware. A user on X posted a screenshot of two sentences allegedly added to the prompt, telling Claude to build original games but not to recreate a specific existing game. Anthropic's Addy Osmani did not confirm that exact wording but said the company added two sentences to the Claude Code prompt on Opus 5.5 to steer it away from recreating someone else's specific game, because using Claude to infringe intellectual property violates its usage policy. He said original games, games in a genre, and games built from users' own designs and assets should keep working, and that Anthropic will keep iterating to strike the right balance. The change matters because AI tools are increasingly used to make game clones, decompilations, and ports, and Anthropic is drawing a line between original game creation and copying existing titles.

OpenAI pitches data center power plants as a way to protect nearby areas

OpenAI announced a pilot called the "Grid Protectors Program" at a planned data center in Effingham County, Georgia, where it says it will free up to 1,000 megawatts of power during extreme weather or emergencies—enough to power air conditioners in about 700,000 homes. OpenAI says its year-round power demand can help spread the grid's fixed costs across more electricity sales, and it is also building "Stargate" data centers with Oracle and SoftBank in Texas and New Mexico.

13 MIT Technology Review

Stop counting tokens, start counting what ships

A single AI coding task can use 10 to 50 times more tokens than a basic chat prompt, because an agent doesn't just answer once — it plans, writes code, runs tests, and repeats the process to fix errors. The article argues that this makes token counts a poor way to measure value, and that people should focus instead on what actually ships. The point matters for developers and companies paying for AI agents, since the more useful an agent becomes, the more calls it makes and the more tokens it burns.
