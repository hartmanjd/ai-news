01 Hacker News

Claude Code sends your email address to websites

A user reported that Claude Code was sending their email address to third-party websites in the User-Agent header during web searches, which they said led to dozens of scam emails per day. The issue was filed on GitHub for macOS on version 2.1.318, and many commenters reacted critically to Anthropic. An Anthropic staff member responded that a built-in privacy plugin called cc-plugin-privacy, enabled by default, blocks this behavior starting in version 2.1.317. Other users then pointed out gaps: the plugin only matches the account email, so requests that include a project name but no address still pass, and one user said the email still leaked on version 2.1.319. The reporter said they were moving their company to Pi with OpenRouter over privacy concerns.

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash

Google has released Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, a new video generation model now generally available through the Gemini API and Gemini for business. 05 TechCrunch

Hundreds of Artex AI hacking tools found publicly exposed to the web

Security researchers at the firm Censys found more than 800 copies of a Chinese-made AI hacking tool called Artex that were publicly accessible on the internet without a password. Artex is open-source software that automatically probes computer systems for weaknesses, and Microsoft has described it as a system of AI agents that can carry out cyberattacks with little human involvement. Censys researcher Silas Cutler said not every exposed copy is run maliciously, but it is unclear why so many are reachable online when the software does not require it. The exposure matters because Artex has been linked to real attacks, including against the Korean finance industry in September and against IT support giant ConnectWise in October, where hackers broke into customers' Microsoft cloud environments and stole data quickly. Many of these attacks exploited simple weaknesses like weak or known passwords and missing multi-factor authentication. Artex has since said it will go closed-source, but copies of its code remain available on code-sharing sites and can still be used in attacks.

09 Ars Technica

“Does Meta think we can’t read?” Meta abandons sketchy commercial terms update.

Meta has indefinitely delayed plans to update its commercial terms, which would have required Meta Business account users to allow extensive AI training on external websites linked on Facebook. A Meta spokesperson told Reuters the changes are paused while the company engages with stakeholders, and existing terms remain in effect. Legal experts had warned that the vague language could let Meta ignore robots.txt scraping preferences and crawl large amounts of off-Facebook content for AI training, potentially avoiding millions in licensing fees from news publishers. Businesses had no way to opt out, and the terms even required publishers to create test credentials so Meta's bots could bypass paywalls. The changes, first set for October 30 and then pushed to November 20, drew pushback from users, courts, and regulators, with courts in Brazil and Norway issuing preliminary injunctions and the UK's Competition and Markets Authority opening a formal investigation.
