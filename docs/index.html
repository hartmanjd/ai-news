01 Hacker News

OpenAI cuts GPT-6.2 usage: Astra hits limits 40% faster, Sol 25% faster

OpenAI said it made a billing mistake when it launched GPT-6.2 that gave most users an incorrect discount on usage of the GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol models. The company has now fixed the error, which means the same work will use up more of a user's plan allowance. Compared with last week, users will reach their limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol. OpenAI said these models should still go further than 6 Astra and 6.1 Sol, that other models were not affected, and that users will not be charged for the difference on past usage. As an apology, OpenAI is adding one banked reset for everyone on the Go, Plus, Pro, and Business plans.

02 Hacker News

Tracking Diseases Cured by AI

A public ledger tracking diseases cured by AI reports that, as of November 26, 2026, the number is zero. No AI-discovered drug has been approved by the FDA or EMA, and no disease has been cured by one. The page notes that 117 AI-enabled drug candidates had entered human trials by July 2025 from 63 companies, and only one has reached Phase III: Rentosertib from Insilico Medicine, an investigational treatment for lung fibrosis that is not a cure. The ledger sets a strict bar for counting a cure, requiring that AI found the drug, regulators approved it, and patients were actually cured, and it excludes things like AI reading scans or predicting protein shapes. It also quotes AI leaders including Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis, and Dario Amodei promising disease cures within roughly a decade, and measures the zero against those claims.

03 Hacker News

OpenAI may be in cost control mode

OpenAI released new versions of its Astra and Sol models, called Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2, and they were widely criticized. Data from a model comparison system shows the new versions use fewer output tokens and reasoning tokens per task than their predecessors. Compared with competing models, Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 reason much less, making them outliers that appear to "underthink." The article reports that these drops are statistically significant. The findings suggest OpenAI tuned the models to save money, which matters because users may get weaker answers in exchange for lower costs.

04 Hacker News

Google Will Make AI Agents Use Its MCP Servers. The Gmail One Can't Send Email

Google has made its eight Workspace MCP servers generally available and added a clause to its API terms requiring any tool that is or is driven by an AI agent to reach Gmail, Drive, Calendar and other Workspace apps through those servers starting 1 February 2027. The Gmail server can search mail, read threads, create drafts and apply labels, but it cannot send, reply, forward or edit drafts, because those tools require partner access and are not generally available. Agents that call the REST APIs directly will be held to Standard-tier quotas regardless of their paid tier, while apps that don't involve AI are unaffected. The addendum also limits Workspace data to active, user-authorized tasks, caps retention of things like vector embeddings and memory indexes at 30 days, and bans training or fine-tuning on the data. Google says the change is needed because a valid OAuth token no longer proves user intent when agents act autonomously, and routing agents through one gateway lets admins block actions, require approval and audit activity. The gap matters because large partners like Anthropic's Claude connector can send and forward email through approved channels, while independent developers using the same scopes can only leave drafts.

05 Hacker News

AI made self-hosting fun again

A long-time self-hoster writes that AI coding agents have renewed his enthusiasm for running his own services. Because his infrastructure configuration lives in Git repositories as Docker Compose files and scripts, he can now use AI agents to make changes with much less effort than before. He says this helped him adopt NixOS more extensively on his home server and build his own Debian-based bootc image, a task he estimates would have taken a week without AI but took half a day with it. He also used AI to help automate build, update, and deployment processes, and he now builds small custom tools for tasks like downloading invoices and Linux ISOs instead of searching for existing software. He notes downsides, including maintenance burden, AI-generated bugs and security issues, and the temptation to deploy things just because it is easy.

06 TechCrunch

Nvidia hits pause on chip shipments to Southeast Asia

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners including Dell and Super Micro to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia for an unspecified period. The company says it is checking whether its regional partners are meeting U.S. export rules. The pause matters because Chinese companies have been using Nvidia chips in data centers in those countries to get around U.S. restrictions. The U.S. Commerce Department closed that loophole earlier this month. A think tank, the Institute for Progress, estimates that remote data centers like these supply about 24% of China's AI computing power.

07 TechCrunch

Grand Theft Auto VI proves why efficiency isn’t a virtue

Grand Theft Auto VI launched last week after a 13-year wait and became the highest-rated game ever on Metacritic, making $3.79 billion on its first day. Rockstar Games made the game entirely without generative AI, with about 5,000 people working on it for more than a decade. The same week, AI company Anthropic went public in an $81.3 billion IPO, the largest ever. Former Take-Two CEO Ben Feder said GTA VI is likely the last game of its kind made this way, because AI-driven development favors speed over the slow, costly process of trial and error. The article argues this matters because it shows what careful human work can achieve at a time when the AI industry treats efficiency as its main goal.

08 The Verge

42 state AGs are trying to stop Character.AI from selling users’ chats

A group of 42 state attorneys general is trying to stop Character.AI from selling user chat data during its Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The attorneys general say the company plans to auction off the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including more than 2.1 million registered to minors, with no safeguards for those users. They say there are no requirements that a buyer honor existing privacy policies, no way for users to delete their data, and no limits on what a buyer could do with it. Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October after years of scandals and legal challenges, and Disney bought some of its assets and hired its CEO. The first bidder for the data was only established on November 13th, 2026, with no parent company or investors listed. Some states, including New York, California, and Colorado, have also passed chatbot laws that Character.AI may violate when they take effect on January 1st, 2027.
