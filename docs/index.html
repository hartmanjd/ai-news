01 Hacker News

LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you

A writer describes using Anthropic's Claude Code to build a tool that identifies songs from Instagram reels and finds the artists' Bandcamp pages so he can buy their music. When Bandcamp added a challenge to its search page, Claude refused to work around it, but the same tool would still download songs from YouTube, which sends no money to the artist. The author argues this shows AI agents answer to their parent companies rather than their users, since Anthropic decides what the model will and won't help with. He worries this control could extend to paid product placement in answers or to following government demands on copyright. He concludes that locally run open models are the way to get an agent that works for its owner.

02 Hacker News

Tidal bans AI-generated music

Tidal announced it will ban music it believes is entirely AI-generated, starting with removals on Dec. 9 and a full clear-out by the beginning of the year. Robert Andersen, the Tidal lead at parent company Block, said 43% of tracks sent to the service in the last 90 days were detected as AI-generated, and that 96% of AI-generated songs get zero plays. The ban will remove 29 million tracks from the platform and reject 80,000 to 85,000 songs a day, making Tidal the first streaming service to ban AI music outright. This follows Tidal's earlier steps to demonetize AI songs and require labels for AI-generated or AI-assisted tracks. Competitors are taking softer approaches: Apple Music plans to label AI tracks, and Spotify labels some artists as an "AI Persona" and has removed tens of millions of spam tracks. The move matters because AI-generated music is growing fast on streaming platforms, and Tidal is pushing other services to follow its lead.

04 Hacker News

GPT-6 models demonstrate intrinsic protein-design capabilities

OpenAI says its GPT-6 family of models can design new protein shapes and amino acid sequences on their own, using reasoning and general-purpose code rather than calling specialized biology models. In a preprint, the company reports that its latest internal models matched or beat specialized models on most protein design tasks, though predicting a complete protein structure remained a clear weakness. In lab tests with research partner Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs showed binding for all five targets tested, and redesigned antibodies kept their binding. Of 322 minibinders tested against five targets, 35 (10.9%) bound as intended, a lower hit rate than an earlier RFdiffusion study, which the authors note depends on filtering and how binding is defined. Specialized models still supplied sequences and helped pick candidates for lab testing. The work matters because a general model that adapts to new instructions through prompting could lower the expertise needed for protein design, but the lab results are early and structure prediction remains a gap.

09 Ars Technica

What's going on with the Riemann hypothesis?

A mathematician named Levent Alpöge, who has been working at Anthropic, announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis that he generated using the AI system Claude, starting it on a simplified "toy" version of his own insights and then directing it to expand to the full problem. The Riemann hypothesis dates from the mid-1800s and concerns the distribution of prime numbers, which gives it importance for number theory and cryptography. The proof exists in two forms: a rigorous version in the Lean programming language, which the Mathlib initiative has confirmed aligns with its longstanding formal statement of the hypothesis, and a description of the logic behind it. Mathematicians are still digesting the result, and an open letter has voiced fears that AI-generated proofs skip the process of working through reasoning, which is often what actually advances the field. The article notes that AI has so far only solved problems humans already recognized as significant, and that the opacity of AI models means a proof may offer little on its own in the way of new ideas.
