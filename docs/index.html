04 TechCrunch

PrismML brings its tiny LLMs to Qualcomm-powered smart glasses

PrismML, an AI lab founded by Caltech researchers and advised by UC Berkeley’s Ion Stoica, has made a version of its tiny language models for smart glasses running on Qualcomm’s Snapdragon chips. On Wednesday, at Qualcomm’s Snapdragon Summit, Qualcomm showed PrismML’s 1-bit Bonsai LLM, which can run locally on AI smart glasses built on the Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 Platform. PrismML says it can shrink larger models by 4x while keeping almost all of their performance on standard benchmarks. The smart glasses version is a 2-billion-parameter model tuned for vision and language, so wearers can ask what they are looking at in real time. PrismML’s larger goal is open-weight AI that runs on devices and makes better use of their existing computing power, as an alternative to relying on proprietary AI labs’ privacy promises and their need for more compute. Releasing a model for Qualcomm’s chip is a step toward that vision, but no smart glasses running PrismML have been announced yet.

05 TechCrunch

Oracle sends force majeure notice on its New Mexico Stargate data center

Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the developer of Project Jupiter, a Stargate data center campus in New Mexico, Bloomberg first reported. The notice could let Oracle delay payments if the campus misses its 2028 target to come online, but Oracle is not seeking to exit as the campus’s main tenant. Oracle said Project Jupiter remains on its planned schedule and that it is fully committed to New Mexico, while Blue Owl Capital, whose unit received the notice, said the notice does not change financial commitments to the multi-year project. The campus has faced setbacks, many tied to its energy supply, including a nearly six-month delay of an Energy Transfer gas pipeline to February 1, 2027, and a pending air-quality permit for its Bloom Energy fuel cells. Project Jupiter is a flagship site of Stargate, the AI infrastructure initiative from Oracle, OpenAI, and SoftBank, and it has drawn opposition from residents and environmental groups ahead of the midterm elections. The notice comes as AI data center construction faces growing scrutiny nationally and developers take on steep costs to build capacity for customers like OpenAI.

Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar gives Google&#8217;s AI a face

Google has launched Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar, an update that lets users talk with the AI while watching an animated persona respond in real time. The Live Avatar can lip-sync and show facial expressions, and it is currently available only to Gemini Enterprise customers. It supports 97 languages and can switch between them without degrading video quality or causing visual drift, and one Google video shows it speaking English and Japanese with matching mouth movements while also showing information on screen. This comes about a week after Google announced the Gemini 3.8 Live model, which it says processes visual inputs in near real-time. Google will offer preset avatars and let organizations create their own, and the avatar’s output includes an invisible SynthID watermark plus safeguards meant to respect identity. The update matters because it gives Google’s AI a visible, talking face for enterprise conversations, though it is not yet open to all users.

09 Ars Technica

Google's first Suncatcher orbital data center test launches October 1

Google is launching its first experimental satellite for Project Suncatcher on October 1. The satellite, called MVP, is about the size of a refrigerator and carries four of Google's custom TPU AI accelerators. It will launch on a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket as part of the Transporter-18 rideshare mission. The spacecraft was built by Planet Labs, while the AI hardware inside is Google's, and its solar panels supply about one kilowatt of power. This test is meant to validate Google's idea of putting AI data centers in orbit, which could help avoid some of the massive power demands of Earth-based data centers. The satellite will operate for just a few months.
