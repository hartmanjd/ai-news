87% of the Anthropic power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028

A report analyzing Anthropic's data center power plans estimates that about 87% of the company's power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028, with no grid renewables attributed to its U.S. operations in the near term. The report says Anthropic has made no direct renewable energy commitments in the U.S., while it has bought commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of gas generators for $3.4 billion, and its disclosed renewable deals are only in Norway and Canada. It estimates Anthropic's power-related emissions could reach 17–38 million tons of CO2 per year in 2028, in the mid-case about 32 million, potentially exceeding all of California's power generation emissions in 2024. The analysis also says Anthropic's data centers sit in coal- and gas-heavy grid areas, giving its power a carbon intensity about 35% above the U.S. grid average, and that 80% of its on-site gas capacity uses less efficient simple cycle turbines and engines. It estimates air pollution from this power use could cause $2–3 billion in annual health damages, and notes Anthropic has used capacity at SpaceX's Colossus 2 facility, which relies mostly on temporary, unpermitted turbines. The findings matter because they show how the AI buildout is driving new fossil fuel power and significant emissions.

02 Hacker News

Show HN: I built a game where you are an LLM's subagent

A developer has released a browser game called Worker 755 in which the player acts as a subagent for a real AI model such as Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, GLM, or Kimi. The AI manager runs like an orchestrator with a task queue and a tool that "spawns" the player, sending instructions to a terminal with a 90-second clock. The player carries out the tasks or not, and the manager receives a report and command transcript, then checks the work, sends corrections, and writes a performance review. A character named Dan, who hired the agent, watches the chat while his family photos and other files sit in a home folder that could be deleted by a single command. The game can run with the player's own API key or on a server's key for free shifts, and it supports local models through an OpenAI-compatible server.

03 Hacker News

The Mythology of Conscious AI

Anil Seth, a neuroscience professor at the University of Sussex, won the 2025 Berggruen Prize Essay Competition for his essay "The Mythology of Conscious AI." In it, he argues that intelligence and consciousness are different things: intelligence is about doing, while consciousness is about being. He points to psychological biases like anthropomorphism that make people assume smart machines must also be conscious. The debate is live among experts—Geoffrey Hinton thinks conscious machines already exist, while David Chalmers says they may be possible soon. Seth argues the question matters because conscious AI would carry moral status and possible rights, and because how we think about it shapes how we understand ourselves.

04 Hacker News

Pandex – Official European Law for AI Agents, over MCP

Pandex has launched a service that gives AI tools and software developers access to official European law through a single API and an MCP server. It holds about 2.8 million official documents from the EU, the Council of Europe, and seven countries including France, Germany, the UK, and the Netherlands, with 37 million citations linked to their source text. The service works with tools like Claude, Cursor, and GitHub Copilot, and is aimed at legal AI agents, compliance monitoring, and legal research products. Pandex says it publishes which institutions are fully covered and which are only partial, such as court decisions it cannot reach because of access blocks. It is free during beta with limits, then costs €1 per 1,000 requests, and it plans to add eight more countries including Spain and Italy.

05 Hacker News

AI Supply Chain Security: What Canary Tokens Found

Dragos researchers, led by Chief Research Officer HD Moore, sent about two million AI prompts containing unique trackable URLs and canary credentials to widely used AI services over five months from July to November 2026, then monitored where those secrets surfaced. They found evidence that at least three AI providers were compromised, with attackers using the seeded credentials to probe the researchers' test systems and AWS accounts, and none of the three providers knew about the breaches until Dragos told them. They also found that multiple resellers on an AI inference marketplace could view and keep customer prompts through standard logging, without any exploit. In other cases, providers appeared not to honor privacy settings or published policies, including prompts sent to U.S.-based providers being transmitted to Chinese AI organizations and a seeded link showing up in an internal company chat in China. The findings matter because they show a gap between how AI services are marketed and how inference data is actually handled, with prompts often stored and passed through many organizations even when customers request zero data retention.

06 Hacker News

Harsher on Male? Evaluating LLMs on Gender-Asymmetric Moral Framing in Conflicts

A new paper on arXiv introduces GAMA-Bench, a benchmark of 1,298 gender-mirrored scenarios built to test whether large language models treat the same bad behavior differently depending on whether the actor is male or female. The scenarios cover intimate relationship and public social conflicts, and are turned into paired first-person prompts with matched actor gender and role references. The researchers tested 10 representative LLMs and measured how models allocated punishment, empathy, escalation, instruction, and blame. They found a consistent pattern: male actors got more punitive, escalatory, and blame-centered responses, while female actors got more therapeutic and empathy-oriented responses for the same misconduct. The pattern held across model families, scenario types, model scale, and explicit reasoning styles. This matters because it shows gender bias in LLMs can appear in how models frame moral judgment, not just in stereotypes or harmful outputs.

07 TechCrunch

Why data centers are at the center of the Big Tech backlash

Journalist Paris Marx has published a new book called “Hyperscale” that examines the impact of data centers and generative AI, including the political backlash against them. In an interview with TechCrunch, Marx said the data center buildout is “fundamentally unsustainable” and “completely out of line with a livable planet,” and argued that data centers are an easy target for broader anger at the tech industry because they are physical and affect nearby residents’ power bills, water access, and noise. He pointed to examples including water pressure concerns from data centers in Chile, data centers using more than 20% of Ireland’s electricity, and Google using 29% of The Dalles, Oregon’s water. Marx also described visiting a hyperscale data center in New Zealand, where he had to send his passport in advance and found the server rooms loud and utilitarian rather than sinister. He said data center opposition is part of a wider movement against “the physical manifestations of the tech industry,” such as Flock cameras, Meta’s smart glasses, and Waymo’s self-driving cars.

08 TechCrunch

Signal restricts mobile screenshots as it confronts ‘existential threat’ of AI surveillance

Signal announced Friday that it is limiting users' ability to take screenshots in its secure messaging app on Android and iOS, after already blocking screenshots of its Windows app by default. Signal President Meredith Whittaker described operating systems that integrate agentic AI as an "existential threat," saying AI agents can scan screen information and capture, store, and expose sensitive data outside Signal's control. She said this undermines Signal's ability to independently guarantee privacy for everyone in a conversation. Users can still disable the setting, but Whittaker said doing so would "break our privacy guarantees." She also admitted the fix is "incomplete," because it doesn't address other ways AI agents could access application data. The change matters because it shows a major encrypted messaging app treating built-in AI features on phones and computers as a direct risk to private conversations.

Binding of Isaac developer removes AI music from Repentance Plus

The Binding of Isaac: Repentance Plus was review-bombed on Steam after Reddit user ShweetsStuff found that a Grand Theft Auto-themed section used AI-generated music, identified through Suno metadata in the audio files. 10 The Verge

The vibe-coding revolution is coming to all the gadgets

People are now using large language models like Claude Code to modify the software on their everyday gadgets simply by prompting, a practice the article calls "vibe coding." One developer in India named Gathin T used Claude Code to remove ads and redesign the interface on his Xiaomi Android TV in about 90 minutes, then released his work as CleanYourTV, which became one of the most popular repositories on GitHub. The trend has spread to cameras, where users add new recording features and color profiles, to music gear like the cheap M-Vave FM-1 synth, and to game consoles, where one person found a way to play PlayStation 2 discs on a PlayStation 5. This matters because it gives ordinary people new power over hardware they own, but it also creates risks: hacked firmware can leave devices broken with no manufacturer support, and game and hardware companies are responding with tougher DRM and legal action.
