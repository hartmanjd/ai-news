01 Hacker News

No AI Slop: A Manifesto

A group of software engineers and AI practitioners at the AIE Code Summit 2026 has published a "No AI Slop" manifesto, modeled on the 2001 Agile Manifesto, that sets out values and resolutions for using AI responsibly in software work. The document defines "slop" as putting less effort into producing something than you ask the reader to spend understanding it, and warns that AI makes this asymmetric because generating a thousand-line pull request or ten-page document costs almost nothing while reviewing it still takes real human time. The signers, including Kent C. Dodds, Tejas Kumar, Lauren Tan, and Swyx, argue that AI amplifies human judgment rather than replacing it, and that slop compounds as agents copy earlier slop from code and knowledge bases. Their resolutions include doing their own thinking before starting work, disclosing when an agent wrote something, reviewing outputs based on stakes, and judging work by outcomes rather than lines of code or tokens spent. The manifesto has 352 signatures so far and its authors say they welcome feedback as the document evolves.

The Claude Delusion

The article argues that wealthy and successful people in tech often come to believe they deserve their success and are smarter than they really are. It uses Bill Gates as an example, citing a New York Times interview where he said he made up advanced biology questions to test OpenAI's models and was shocked they answered them well. 03 Hacker News

Claude Meetings

Anthropic has launched Claude Meetings in beta, letting Claude join video calls as a visible participant that follows the conversation and does work during the meeting. Teams can ask Claude questions out loud or in chat, and it can write notes, action items, docs, and whiteboards while people keep talking, using connected tools only with permission. It is rolling out first on Google Meet for Team and Enterprise plans, with other platforms coming soon. By default, Claude stays out of calls with people from outside the organization unless an admin allows it, and it saves a transcript, summary, and screen descriptions but not audio. The feature matters because it aims to take over meeting notes and follow-up work, and Anthropic is partnering with tools like Gong, Otter, and Fireflies.ai to send transcripts to software teams already use. During the beta, notes and transcripts are free, and it is not available to some organizations, including those with zero data retention or HIPAA-ready setups.

04 Hacker News

Anthropic upgrades Claude Code Projects to run parallel AI workers

Anthropic has redesigned Projects in Claude Code so that users describe a goal and Claude manages the work itself, splitting it across parallel threads that each run as a separate cloud session. Claude scopes the request, delegates tasks, coordinates the threads, reviews outputs, and assembles the result, and users can steer progress from their phone or step away while it continues. For example, Claude can profile endpoints and open pull requests in parallel to cut latency, or create a thread per repo to migrate callers off a deprecated endpoint. The updated projects are available in beta to select Claude Pro and Max subscribers using cloud sessions, with wider access rolling out over the coming week and to Team and Enterprise plans after that. Existing projects keep working as they do today. Projects also build shared memory over time, so Claude remembers project details and your working style, which matters because these long-running workflows can hit usage limits faster.

05 Hacker News

Show HN: DeepSeek V3 in ~200 lines of pure Jax

A developer has released a small, readable implementation of the DeepSeek-V3 architecture in about 190 lines of pure JAX code, without using Flax, Equinox, or PyTorch. The model was pretrained from scratch on a MacBook Air using public-domain detective fiction, with 36.2 million parameters and about 21.7 million training tokens. The code reproduces key ideas from the DeepSeek-V3 technical report, including Multi-head Latent Attention, DeepSeekMoE routing, auxiliary-loss-free balancing, and multi-token prediction. In a test, the balancing bias kept expert load even across experts without changing validation loss, matching the paper's claim. The author describes it as an architecture demo rather than a strong language model, and notes it leaves out features like FP8 training and sparse expert dispatch.

06 TechCrunch

The AI graveyard: a running list of projects and startups that didn’t make it

A growing number of AI products and startups have shut down or been abandoned, creating what the article calls an "AI graveyard." Character.ai, founded by former Google researchers, filed for bankruptcy in October after raising $150 million and reaching a $1 billion valuation, facing lawsuits over teen harm and a Disney cease-and-desist letter. Privacy-focused assistant Ollie announced its shutdown on November 6, with its CEO citing an inability to stand out, grow, or raise enough capital. According to S&P Global Market Intelligence, about 42% of AI initiatives are ultimately abandoned by their corporate parents, due to insufficient funding, technical challenges, competition, or weak demand. Even OpenAI has had missteps, including a confusing ChatGPT redesign it quickly rolled back and the shutdown of stand-alone products like its Atlas browser and Sora video platform. The article frames these failures as lessons for the rest of the industry rather than a simple scorecard of flops.

07 TechCrunch

Asian governments react to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s exit

Jensen Huang left Nvidia after the board launched an independent investigation into reports that he was targeted by a Chinese spy operation; Huang also admitted to an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based chip company Megaspeed. China's foreign ministry rejected the spy allegations as groundless and said it opposed export controls that disrupt global supply chains. Huang's exit raised uncertainty over more than $3 billion in Chinese AI computing projects that rely on Nvidia's restricted processors, according to Reuters. In Singapore, authorities had seized Alice Huang's passport in June 2025 during an investigation into possible false declarations about server purchases; police later found insufficient evidence of related offenses but said she knowingly gave inaccurate information about her role at Megaspeed, and she was warned and barred from re-entering Singapore. South Korea's Science Ministry said Nvidia reaffirmed plans to supply 260,000 advanced GPUs and continue joint AI projects, while Taiwan's economy minister said the departure was unlikely to significantly change global AI industry growth and Taipei said Nvidia's planned overseas headquarters there remained on track. Japan's Industry Ministry said it would closely monitor Nvidia, calling the company an important player in Japan's AI semiconductor industry.

08 The Verge

The OpenAI board member who fired Sam Altman says we still can&#8217;t trust AI CEOs

Helen Toner, executive director of Georgetown’s Center for Security and Emerging Technology, was one of four OpenAI board members who voted to fire CEO Sam Altman, a move that ended with Altman reinstated, Toner resigning, and OpenAI restructuring. She said the nonprofit board was meant to ensure the company did not put profit and growth ahead of its mission to build safe AI, but that accountability mechanism broke when the board tried to use it. Toner argued policymakers want independent, rigorous information on AI, and that heavy industry spending in the midterm elections did not succeed in pushing out candidates who talk about AI safety and regulation.

09 The Verge

Jensen Huang and the decline of the visionary showman founder CEO

Jensen Huang has left Nvidia, the company he cofounded 33 years ago, after reportedly enabling two chip smuggling scandals and allegedly having three extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. Huang was the CEO of the first $5 trillion company, which makes the GPUs that power the worldwide AI boom and represents about 6 percent of the S&P 500 index on its own. He had no deputies and around 60 employees reported directly to him, and he did not plan for succession. Analysts say he was the closest thing tech has had to Steve Jobs, both a visionary and a tactical leader. His departure matters because Nvidia's chips are central to the US AI industry, and the world is watching whether the scandal could disrupt that industry or destabilize the stock market.

10 Ars Technica

FAA expands AI advice to manage air traffic congestion across entire US airspace

The Federal Aviation Administration has expanded its AI tool called SMART to help manage air traffic across all 22 air traffic control centers in the US, after an initial rollout in the Washington, DC area on September 21. The FAA says the $875 million system helped reroute 180 flights there, saving an average of 10 minutes and almost 80 gallons of fuel per flight, and it hopes to repeat those results nationwide. SMART is a cloud-based system that uses weather, flight path, traffic and staffing data to predict traffic patterns and recommend schedules and routes. Airline executives support the rollout, but the union representing air traffic controllers says it was not involved in designing or testing the system. A team of FAA specialists in DC must still validate SMART's recommendations before they reach controllers.
