02 Hacker News

Gemini 4 Hydrogen

Google has released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster, lower-cost model in its Gemini 4 family, designed for everyday coding and agentic workflows rather than the deep reasoning handled by its siblings Carbon and Argon. Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, slightly above Argon's 50, and is priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across its 1 million-token context window. Google is also introducing Orchestrator in Google Antigravity, which automatically switches between models like Carbon and Hydrogen within a session, reaching near-Carbon task success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster. Compared with Gemini 3.8 Flash, Hydrogen completes tasks 2.5 times faster at its default setting and more than triples its Terminal-Bench 4.1 score (57% vs. 18.2%). The model is rolling out across Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, the Gemini app, Google Search, and paid Google AI Pro and Ultra tiers.

03 Hacker News

I'm making LLMs work on small and old devices

A project called smolmaxxing has released patches to llama.cpp that let large language models run on very small, old devices. The author tested it on a Xiaomi TV Box S (3rd Gen) with about 1 GB of usable RAM and a jailbroken 2013 iPad Air with about 700 MB usable, running models up to 16B parameters by keeping only part of each model in memory and streaming the rest from flash storage. On the TV box, a 16B Ling-mini-2.0 model ran at 5.1 tokens per second, and the iPad ran the same model at 2.6–4.5 tokens per second. The techniques include custom kernels for the chips, dropping rarely used vocabulary rows to save RAM, and paging mixture-of-experts weights from flash on demand. The code is MIT-licensed and the author says the methods should work on other Linux or Android devices with more model than RAM.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen and Orchestrator

Google has rolled out Gemini 4 Hydrogen inside its Antigravity developer platform, along with a new model option called Orchestrator. Orchestrator pairs Hydrogen with Gemini 4 Carbon, switching between the two models as a task changes: Carbon plans and fixes problems, while Hydrogen writes edits and runs tests. Google says this approach reached near-Carbon task success on Terminal-Bench 4.1 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster. Hydrogen also works on its own for everyday coding, scoring 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1. Hydrogen is rolling out to paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, while Orchestrator is rolling out to Ultra subscribers, and both are available to enterprises through Gemini for business.

07 The Verge

No, AI agents did not discover a previously unknown Maya site

Google researcher Pietro Miotti claimed this week that a team of AI agents had discovered a possibly unmapped Maya site in Mexico, but the site had already been documented in an academic journal more than a year earlier. Miotti, who said the project was a personal side project done without Google resources, used 48 AI agents to scan NASA lidar data and then asked Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History whether the site was known; the institute said it had no record of it. Archaeologists criticized the claim online, noting that a site not being in a government database does not mean it is unknown, and that local communities often already know about such sites. Ivan Šprajc, a coauthor of the paper that documented the site, said archaeologists have used lidar for years and that "just spotting something does not add much to our knowledge." Miotti acknowledged he is not an archaeologist and updated his post to credit the earlier work. The incident highlights a growing tension as amateurs use powerful AI tools in specialist fields without the expertise to check existing research.
