02 Hacker News

How should an LLM see a computer?

A team tested how different OpenAI models perform computer tasks on the OSWorld benchmark, comparing three setups: screenshots alone, screenshots plus an accessibility tree (a text description of on-screen elements), and clicking by element ID. Weaker models improved a lot with the tree and ID clicks—GPT-5.2 went from 15.8% with screenshots alone to 52.8% with both additions, and GPT-4.1 from 5.4% to 29.3%. The strongest models barely changed: GPT-6.1 Sol scored 83.8% with screenshots alone and about 82% with the tree or ID clicks. The authors say the accessibility tree, originally built decades ago for blind users, still helps agents that struggle with clicking exact pixels, but strong models don't need it. They used these results to decide how their QA agent TREX should see the screen: it relies on screenshots at every step and uses the tree to click by ID when possible.

03 TechCrunch

Anthropic launches Claude Ship, letting AI agents review, fix, and approve code changes

Anthropic launched Claude Ship, a set of AI agents that review, fix, and approve routine code changes. The tool includes Code Review, which is generally available, plus two beta agents: PR Steward, which handles failing checks, merge conflicts, and review feedback, and Claude Approvals, which approves changes automatically based on policies a team writes in natural language. The launch addresses a growing bottleneck: AI agents now generate large amounts of code, making human review and approval slower. Anthropic says teams still control where the tool runs, which review settings to use, and which changes go to a human. Claude Ship is rolling out to Claude Team and Enterprise plans for GitHub repositories, and Anthropic suggests starting approvals in observe-only mode.

04 TechCrunch

Supertake raises $20M to let AI agents turn your market predictions into stock portfolios

Supertake, an AI investing startup, has raised $20 million in seed funding led by Adjacent, with participation from Coinbase Ventures, Union Square Ventures, and angel investors including Nat Friedman and Guillermo Rauch. The service lets users describe their market views in plain language, and AI agents turn those ideas into real stock portfolios, currently through Coinbase and Robinhood. The company was incubated at USV by Michael Mignano, and its CEO Lincoln Murr and CTO John Peterson both came from Coinbase, where they led AI product and engineering. Supertake says it adds guardrails such as spend limits, trade size caps, and restrictions that keep AI agents from seeing brokerage credentials or moving funds without consent. The funding follows a $2 million pre-seed round, and the service remains behind a waitlist while in beta. The startup is betting that people will trust AI to manage real money as frontier models improve, a step beyond traditional robo-advisors.
