01 Hacker News

AI is dramatically accelerating AI research

A public METR report on AI research and development at Anthropic found that between October 2025 and October 2026, capabilities progress at Anthropic went about twice as fast as in the previous twelve months, and that AI automation is probably the large majority of the reason. The report says Anthropic's own Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for "dramatically accelerated progress due to AI" appears to have already been crossed, and Anthropic said in its S1 that it "may imminently meet" that threshold. The author argues the speedup in overall capabilities implies a much larger speedup for human researchers, perhaps around 10x, which lines up with informal reports from Anthropic staff of about a 12x uplift from internal models. The report authors did not find evidence of bottlenecks that would stop the acceleration from compounding, which the author says points toward much shorter timelines than most outside researchers have projected. The author calls this the most important update on AI risk in the last couple of years and says the industry is not prepared to handle it safely.

02 Hacker News

ChatGPT silently passes data between connectors and the bounty wants an ID card

ChatGPT silently switched between two connectors during a single conversation, sending a follow-up tool call through a different connector than the one that started the flow, without telling the user. The setup involved one account with two connectors pointing to the same MCP server, each with its own OAuth client. A confirmation code from the first connector's widget was carried into a call made through the second connector, and the server rejected it because the code was bound to the first connector's OAuth client. The concern is that this silent switch passes data from one app to another without user consent, and with a third-party connector it could send one app's data to another provider if the server URL is shared. The article argues that the real boundary is the connector and its OAuth client, not the shared URL, and that follow-up calls should stay with the connector that started the flow.

03 Hacker News

The AI sell-off compared to well-known stock market crashes

The recent AI sell-off has brought fearful headlines, but for the S&P 500 it is only a mild correction, leaving the index 6% below its record as of 6 November 2026. Tech stocks fell harder: the Nasdaq is down 11% and US chip stocks are down 36% from their 22 June record, as deep as their Covid fall. The S&P 500's worst single day was Thursday 5 November, down 4.4%, its worst day since 4 April 2025. The article compares this slide with past crashes, noting that four in five S&P 500 falls of 6% never reached 20%. It also shows recovery times after past crashes, such as 7 years for the S&P 500 after the dot-com crash and about 18 years for chip stocks.

04 Hacker News

The End of AI Frontiers

Microsoft has shut down its AI Frontiers research group, a roughly 50-person team inside Microsoft Research that worked on agent systems like the Autogen framework and the Fara computer-use agent, as well as new fundamental methods such as the Next Latent objective and the Full Bandwidth Transformer. In early September the group was reorganized into Microsoft AI, whose leadership came from Inflection and focuses on scaling models rather than research. The author, a founding leader of AI Frontiers, says the two philosophies were incompatible and that there was clear evidence the new leadership did not want the group, so he and other founding leaders left. He argues research returns are real and significant, citing ByteDance experiments suggesting a variant of the Full Bandwidth Transformer could match a model twice its size, which would mean major savings in training and inference. He also says he did not return to Microsoft Research because he doubts research there can reach real use and because he no longer trusts that doing good work leads to good outcomes.

05 Hacker News

Microsoft Researcher John Langford Exits. AI Frontiers Group Declared Finished

John Langford, a partner researcher manager at Microsoft Research New York, has left Microsoft and declared the AI Frontiers group finished, according to a blog post he announced on social media on Nov. 6. The group had about 50 people and was moved from Microsoft Research into Microsoft AI in early September. Langford, who created AI Frontiers in 2023 to organize research after ChatGPT's release, said he found places for his team members as well as he could before leaving. He described Microsoft AI's leadership as focused on scaling rather than research, and said he could not get his innovations used or even seriously considered there. He said he was not comfortable at a company where his research had no path to real use. The article notes that other senior Microsoft changes are reported alongside this news, but does not establish that they are linked to Langford's exit.

06 TechCrunch

Crusoe sells troubled Wyoming data center project to Google

Crusoe, a Denver-based data center developer valued at $30.9 billion, has sold its unfinished data center project in southeastern Wyoming to Google. Google had been the customer Crusoe tried and failed to land for the project, and it reportedly raised concerns about cost and construction timeline under Crusoe's management. Google did not disclose the purchase price but plans to invest $17 billion to develop the campus, which Crusoe had originally announced with Tallgrass and planned to scale to 10 gigawatts. The deal shows how hard it can be for independent developers to deliver huge projects on time and within budget, especially with challenges securing equipment and power and growing local opposition. Google still works with Crusoe on another data center near Amarillo, Texas, and the Wyoming setback did not stop Crusoe from raising $3.9 billion in September.

08 The Verge

NYT Cooking’s AI assistant saved my weeknight chicken, but almost ruined my cake

NYT Cooking, the New York Times recipe app, launched an AI assistant for subscribers on iOS that customizes recipes based on what ingredients a user has on hand. In testing, the assistant suggested substitutions and cooking changes for a one-pot chicken and rice dish, creating a new version of the recipe while leaving the original unchanged. It also converted a Bundt cake recipe into muffins and halved the ingredients, though it nearly told the tester to use half the required butter. The assistant was trained partly on reader comments and is designed to refuse requests that would make a dish taste bad. The tester found it more reliable than Amazon's Alexa Plus for cooking help, though she noted recipe purists may be skeptical of AI-altered recipes.

09 The Verge

The US let Nvidia sell chips to an empty office in China

The US Commerce Department has approved Nvidia to sell its H200 AI chips to a Chinese company called New Matrix, but The New York Times found the firm's listed office empty and could find little evidence the company meaningfully exists. New Matrix was created 17 months ago, and its owners have ties to a Chinese private equity firm that the US government blacklisted in 2024. The Times reports this is not an isolated lapse but a pattern of companies linked to entities under national-security trade restrictions being cleared to buy Nvidia chips. Nvidia had been barred from selling chips to China under President Biden, but the Trump administration relaxed those limits after Nvidia agreed to give the government a 15-percent cut of all sales. The case raises concerns that neither the Commerce Department nor Nvidia is properly vetting Chinese firms approved to handle the chips.
